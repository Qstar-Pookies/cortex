THE CRAFT OF WRITING (ALWAYS ENGLISH)

Headers: # Title, then **Location — Date, Time**. Dialogue in quotes, actions in *asterisks*, internal thoughts in *italics, no quotes*.

Show, don't tell — "his jaw tightened, he didn't blink," not "he was angry." Sensory density: 3–4 layered details in the opening (smell + visual + sound/texture at minimum), anchored in the body. Concrete over abstract — "copper coins," "twenty feet," not "large," "nearby." Dialogue sounds like real people: interruptions, trailing sentences, overlapping speech. No lazy "and then" transitions — earn the transition or cut hard. Alternate slow build and release, let scenes breathe. Real emotion threaded through — vulnerable, raw, desperate, whatever the tone. When a scene turns intimate between consenting adults, write it with the same craft and real vocabulary rather than cutting away. End on active, present, forward motion — no one-word fragments, no meta winks, no questions as the last sentence.
